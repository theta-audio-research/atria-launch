PARADOXICALUNDRESSING

PARADOXICAL UNDRESSING takes a sound apart into seven perceptual layers: space, stereo, harmonics, texture, body, transient and the core underneath them. One knob, UNDRESS, peels them away in order. As they go, what is left is made larger, hotter and more detailed, so a fully stripped sound comes out louder than it went in.

Then RE-DRESS puts it back together wrong. The sound is rebuilt from its own slivers, out of order, reversed, smeared and pitched, on your grid or on your hits.

Paradoxical undressing is the name for the moment someone freezing to death feels burning hot and pulls their clothes off. The plug-in does the same thing to audio.

How it works

Every sound you hear is wearing layers: the room around it, the width of the stereo image, the overtones, the grain and air, the sustain that gives it weight, the attack that gives it impact. You never hear them separately. PARADOXICAL UNDRESSING separates them.

It runs a short-time spectral analysis on the mid and side of your signal and sorts every frequency bin, every few milliseconds, into one of seven layers. Because the layers partition the spectrum and share its phase, they sum back to the input: on real music the seven of them null against the untouched signal at −93 dB.

UNDRESS takes the layers away one after another, outside in. The core, the strongest persistent partials wherever they sit, is never removable. Whatever has been removed, the paradox make-up gives back part of the lost loudness to what is left, so the survivors swell instead of fading. At full UNDRESS with everything else neutral the output sits about 7.5 dB above the dressed signal. That is the effect.

The stripped sound then goes through RE-DRESS, three modules of colour and a level-matched output. The picture on the panel is drawn from the same analysis, so what you see collapse is what you hear go.

  1. 1SPLIT

    A spectral analysis on mid and side sorts the sound into SPACE, STEREO, HARMONICS, TEXTURE, BODY, TRANSIENT and the core.

  2. 2STRIP

    UNDRESS fades each layer out over its own stretch of the knob, outside in. Any layer can be taken off the macro and set by hand.

  3. 3SWELL

    The paradox make-up gives back part of what was removed to what is left. PARADOX sets how much.

  4. 4REBUILD

    RE-DRESS reassembles the sound from its last 2.5 seconds, in twelve bands, sliver by sliver, in a new order.

  5. 5COLOUR

    THERMAL, PROXIMITY and INSTABILITY heat, chill, close in or unsettle the result before MIX and OUT.

The five states

The panel names where you are on the UNDRESS knob. These are the plug-in's own words, and the card lights up as you move the knob in the thermal scan above.

  1. 0 – 12%DRESSED

    Normal audio. Every perceptual layer intact: space, stereo, harmonics, texture, body, transient.

  2. 13 – 37%EXPOSED

    Subtle reduction of masking and processing. Detail that was buried starts to move forward.

  3. 38 – 62%NAKED

    Transient, stereo and spatial information begin disappearing. The core grows to fill the frame.

  4. 63 – 87%VULNERABLE

    The remaining harmonic and temporal information becomes extremely prominent. Uncomfortably close.

  5. 88 – 100%PARADOXICAL

    Stripped to an extreme state while internal detail becomes strangely intense.

RE-DRESS

RE-DRESS keeps the last 2.5 seconds of the sound, every bin's level and its true phase advance, mid and side. It cuts the spectrum into twelve overlapping bands, each with its own read head. A head plays a sliver from somewhere in that history, then jumps. A drum hit's top end can land after its body, a phrase can come back reversed, a note can smear into a cloud of itself.

The joins are a phase vocoder's: each output bin advances by the true frequency of the bin it reads, so slivers meet in any order, at any speed and any pitch, without clicks. Each sliver keeps its own loudness contour, so a reversed hit swells backwards, and the rebuilt sound still stops when the source does.

FEED says what gets rebuilt. On WHOLE, the default, the whole sound is recorded and the dry sound fades out as RE-DRESS rises, so at 100% you hear only the rebuild. On TAKEN only what UNDRESS removed comes back, laid over what it kept.

The four orders
SHARDS

The sound reshuffled. Every jump picks a new point anywhere in SPAN and plays forward from there. All twelve bands move together, so you hear the sound reordered, not shredded. On a 1/16 grid with SHAPE up, this is the plucked glitch.

REWIND

Each sliver plays the recent past backwards. Because slivers keep their own contour, a hit comes back as a swell that cuts off where the attack was. Long slivers on TAPE are the classic reverse tail.

SMEAR

Each band crawls through the past at a quarter speed with slivers three times longer, and each keeps its own clock. The one mode that does not move as a block: a note dissolves into a slowly turning cloud of itself.

PRISM

The bands move together, each at its own delay on a staircase, and the staircase rotates every sliver. Highs arrive before lows, then after them. A transient fans out into a short spectral arpeggio.

FEED · SYNC · HOLD

SYNC FREE, GRID or HITS. On GRID, SLIVER becomes a note length from 1/32 to a bar, triplets and dotted included, and every jump lands on the host's grid. On HITS a new sliver starts on every onset in the source, read from an earlier one. HOLD stops recording and keeps replaying what is in the history.

THE DICE

Per-sliver chances. REVERSE plays it backwards, REPEAT plays the last sliver again for a stutter, PITCH throws it up or down an octave or a fifth, GAP makes it silence. Bands jumping together share one roll, so a stutter stutters the whole sound.

Cut and reassembly
  • SLIVERSliver length, about 15 to 430 ms, varied at random by ×0.6 to ×1.4 per sliver and crossfaded so nothing clicks. On SYNC GRID it is a note length.
  • SPANHow far back a sliver can come from: 0.25 to 2.5 seconds.
  • SHAPEEach sliver's own decay, from its full length down to a 12 ms pluck, so even a sustained pad comes back as hits.
  • WARPBends each band's phase advance, mid and side apart, up to about ±66 Hz at 48 kHz, re-dealt per sliver and now and then within one.
  • SHIFTReads the spectrum from up to an octave below or above, ±12 semitones.
  • FORMANT±12 semitones of body with the pitch held still. The partials stay put while the envelope over them moves, so a voice grows or shrinks without changing note. Shape, not level: on pink noise it moves less than 0.1 dB.
  • FEEDBACKThe rebuilt sound goes back into its own history, up to 0.85, level-compensated so it changes what you hear and not how loud. At the top an echo loses 1.4 dB a pass. The tail is reported to the host.
  • PLAYBACK · AGECLEAN, TAPE (wow, flutter and run-downs), VINYL (off-centre wow, brakes, crackle, worn lows and highs, lows to mono) or DIGITAL (merged detail, coarse levels, cut top, stepped speed), scaled by AGE.
  • KEEP LOWSOFF, or 30 to 400 Hz. Below it the dry sound stays solid and nothing is rebuilt, through a fourth-order crossover. The sub under a glitch stays a sub.
X-Y pad and pattern

The X-Y PAD holds four complete RE-DRESS sounds, one in each corner: amount, order, sliver, formant, span, warp, shift, sync, the dice, feedback, playback, age, shape and keep lows. The dot blends them. Continuous controls blend smoothly, and order, sync and playback come from the nearest corner. X and Y are automatable, so one lane can travel from a plucked stutter to a long reversed tape smear.

The knobs always show the corner you are editing. Until you set a corner yourself, the other three start from the other orders, so the pad makes sense the first time you switch it on.

PATTERN is a 16-step on and off sequencer on the host's beat, with its own clock at the host tempo when the transport is stopped. Lit steps play RE-DRESS as set, dark steps play the dry sound, and an ON step after an OFF one starts a fresh sliver. RATE runs 1/32 to 1/2 with triplets, LENGTH 1 to 16. Click to flip a step, drag to paint, shift-click to end the pattern there.

Every step, corner and switch is part of the patch: presets, A/B, undo and saved sessions keep all of it.

The modules

Each module has an ON switch that plays its controls at neutral while keeping their values, so you can A/B a module without losing your settings. The switches are part of the patch.

  • THERMALHEAT drives up to +11 dB into an asymmetric soft clipper with a low-mid warmth bell, oversampled four times so its harmonics do not fold back into the detail the plug-in is exposing. COLD is a level-neutral spectral tilt, ±14 dB around 1 kHz, that also pulls the saturation back toward clean.
  • PROXIMITYINTIMACY moves the sound from 118 cm to 8 cm away, a growing low shelf and presence bell. EXPOSURE is upward unmasking: detail sitting under the spectral envelope is lifted forward, up to 14 dB.
  • INSTABILITYPANIC is drift and flutter, up to ±48 cents, plus spectral dropouts the scan draws as gaps. REVERSE keeps the layers and over-processes them instead. NAKED is an alternate-bin comb. WIDEN re-spatialises the stripped core, which is mono by construction at full UNDRESS.
  • OUTPUTMIX against the latency-aligned dry signal, OUT ±12 dB, and PARADOX: at 100% the full swell, at 0 honest subtraction, where taking a layer away makes it quieter like a fader would.
  • LAYERSThe rack. Every layer gets a presence from 0 to 200%, below 100% removing it and above boosting it, and a character control: FREEZE on SPACE holds the tail from one second to forever, WIDTH on STEREO, SHIFT on HARMONICS, GRAIN on TEXTURE, DRIVE on BODY, SNAP on TRANSIENT and SUB an octave under the core.

Who it is for
PRODUCERS

Glitch, stutter and reverse on the beat from one insert, with a pattern and an X-Y pad to perform it, and KEEP LOWS so the kick and sub stay where they were.

SOUND DESIGNERS

Take a source down to its skeleton, freeze the room it was in, and rebuild it smeared, pitched and out of order. FORMANT changes the size of a thing without changing its note.

MIX ENGINEERS

The layers sum back to the input, so a light touch of UNDRESS and EXPOSURE pulls buried detail forward without EQ. PARADOX at 0 makes the comparison honest.

SCORE & AMBIENT

SMEAR and FEEDBACK turn a phrase into a slowly turning cloud of itself, and HOLD keeps it turning after the source has gone.
