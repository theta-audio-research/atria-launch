Subharmonic generator · Low-end instrument

BARYON

BARYON listens to the bass you have and plays the bass underneath it. A three-band generator tracks the low end and divides it down an octave, a third or a fourth. A second engine, modelled on the NEVE DFC console's synthesizer, finds the true fundamental of a bass line and plays any interval from unison to two octaves below it.

Around the generators sit the tools that make new low end sit in a mix: PUNCH and TONE, an EDGE saturator for the sub alone, a beat-locked LFO, dynamic EQ before and after, ANANKE compression, a gate and a true look-ahead limiter.

How it works

A bass note's lowest partial is often weak, and on small speakers it is gone entirely. EQ cannot fix that, because a boost can only raise what is already there. A subharmonic generator makes new low end: it follows the pitch of what you play and synthesises a tone below it, so the bass gets weight it never had.

The hard part is following the pitch. Bass notes are rich in harmonics, and a strong second harmonic can fool a tracker into reading the note an octave high. BARYON gives you two generators that answer this in different ways, and you pick one per instance.

INPUT EQ first focuses what the generator hears. The 3-BAND engine or the ROOT engine then generates the sub, PUNCH and TONE shape it, EDGE saturates it, and SUB LEVEL, WIDTH and the LFO set how it moves. The sub joins the focused source, MIX blends that against your untouched input, and the finishing chain handles the rest.

After the mix come OUTPUT EQ, the ANANKE compressor, master SAT with ROUND and IRON, a GATE, and a look-ahead LIMITER. Mono and stereo tracks both work, and BASS MONO keeps the generated sub in the centre.

  1. 1FOCUS

    INPUT EQ's high-pass, low-pass and dynamic bell decide which part of the low end the generator listens to.

  2. 2TRACK

    3-BAND follows each band's zero crossings. ROOT measures the fundamental with a YIN detector.

  3. 3GENERATE

    A tone below the note, locked to it. Its level follows the note, and the sub gate stops it droning on noise.

  4. 4SHAPE

    PUNCH adds a resonant knee, TONE rolls off the top, EDGE adds harmonics to the sub alone.

  5. 5FINISH

    Output EQ, compression, saturation, gate and a ceiling, then OUT TRIM. BYPASS stays time-aligned.

The classic generator

The 3-BAND engine works like the classic hardware boxes. Each band band-passes its own slice of the low end, between a LOW and a HIGH frequency you set, and watches that slice cross zero. Every rising crossing is one cycle of the note. Count them and you can divide.

DIVIDE is the dbx divider: a flip-flop toggles on each crossing, so its square wave runs at exactly half the note. SYNTH plays a clean sine at the note divided by the band's RATIO (an octave, a third or a fourth), snapped to the crossings so it stays locked like the divider does. HYBRID blends the two: 55 % sine for body, 45 % square for grit.

The sub's level follows the band's own envelope, so it swells and dies with the note. A SUB GATE keeps it silent until the band is really playing, so noise and bleed never drone. When a new hit lands, the band resets its phase, so identical hits give identical subs. On a steady kick loop the difference between hit three and hit four measures 0.000000.

The narrow band is what keeps the lock. In the lab below, open the band's HIGH edge past the second harmonic and push H2 up: the crossings double, and the divider jumps up an octave. That is the problem the ROOT engine was built to solve.

Find the fundamental

Switch the generator to ROOT and BARYON becomes a pitch-tracking synthesizer, modelled on the subharmonic synthesizer in the NEVE DFC film console and the Bass Character section of Harrison's Bass Flow. Two bands each have a sweepable CAPTURE centre from 15 to 160 Hz and a WIDTH from one to twelve semitones either side.

Instead of counting crossings, each band runs a YIN detector. It compares a whole frame of the bass with itself at every lag and picks the first lag where the signal truly repeats. A strong second harmonic makes extra crossings, but it does not make the waveform repeat any sooner, so YIN reads the fundamental where a zero-crossing tracker reads the harmonic.

Each band plays a sine at any whole semitone from 0 to −24 below the live fundamental. At 0 it reinforces the note itself, the Bass Flow "Body" move. At −12 it is an octave down, at −24 two. Because the tone follows the actual note, it never fights the source the way a fixed EQ boost at the wrong frequency can.

The detector searches wider than it accepts. A note outside the capture band reads as unvoiced, so the band holds or gates instead of jumping an octave. In the plug-in's own test, a 50 Hz note with a 100 Hz second harmonic at 78 % of its level gives a 25 Hz sub 78 dB above any 50 Hz error.

Edge and colour

A pure sine an octave below a bass note is felt more than heard, and on a laptop it is not heard at all. EDGE saturates the generated sub, and only the sub, so it grows harmonics that let small speakers suggest the note. DRIVE runs from 0 to 24 dB into one of six curves.

The same six curves drive the master SAT stage later in the chain, with its own DRIVE and MIX and oversampling at OFF, 2× or 4×. EDGE always runs with antiderivative anti-aliasing; the master stage uses it at 4×, where it costs only half a decibel at 20 kHz.

Symmetric curves (TANH, ARCTAN, TAPE, HARD) make odd harmonics. Asymmetric curves (TUBE, DIODE) add even ones. INVERT mirrors an asymmetric curve, so the even-harmonic emphasis moves to the other half of the wave and the tone swings from forward to round. On a symmetric curve there is nothing to mirror, so INVERT switches to a soft asinh rounding instead.

In the plug-in, TUBE also lets its bias sag with sustained level and clamps its crest like a conducting grid, and TAPE is flux-weighted so lows saturate first. ROUND blends in the asinh curve after SAT; IRON adds output-transformer colour.

Motion

The LFO moves the level of the generated sub, so the low end can breathe, pulse or pump in time while your source stays still. Put it in the POST position and it moves the whole processed signal instead, after the compressor, so the compressor cannot flatten the movement, and still before the limiter.

With SYNC on, the LFO's phase is worked out from the host's song position every block, so it stays on the beat through loops and jumps.

  • SHAPESSINE · TRI · SAW · SQUARE · S&H
  • RATE0.02 to 8 Hz free, or SYNC at 1/1, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 or 1/16 of the host tempo
