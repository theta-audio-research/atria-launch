Three engines

Choose the circuit, not the stage count.

Switching engine takes effect on the next block, and the display redraws to match. OPTICAL’s four stage marks fan out up the frequency axis where JFET’s sit together. DUAL gains a second machine sheet and carries up to six notch rows where the others carry two.

JFET

The one your ear supplies.

Four matched first-order allpass stages and a smooth sine sweep. With WARMTH up, the stage coefficient is mildly signal-dependent, so hard playing pulls the sweep slightly lower and wider instead of holding one fixed width. The circuit inside the small orange box that was most people’s first phaser.

OPTICAL

Never the same way twice.

Four stagger-tuned stages driven through a lamp and a photocell rather than a clean LFO. The lamp warms with a 45 ms time constant and cools with a 120 ms one; each stage’s cell lags slightly differently, and WARMTH widens how differently. The sweep rises a little faster than it falls, which a sine cannot manage.

DUAL

Twelve poles, deliberately.

Two six-stage machines in series per channel, their LFOs a fixed 12 degrees apart, each with its own feedback loop, limiter and damping. Parked at CENTER 640, its six notches spread from 84 Hz to 4.7 kHz, where JFET’s two sit at 265 Hz and 1.5 kHz.

The controls

Slow is the signature.

MIX, REGEN, WARMTH and BODY glide over 20 ms, so every control automates without steps. Double-click a knob to return it to its default, or double-click its value to type a number.

RATE0.01 to 10 Hz, weighted so most of the throw lives in the slow half. This is the hero control because slow is the signature.
ULTRA SLOWDivides the rate by twenty. At the bottom of the range that is one sweep every 33 minutes: movement you feel across an arrangement rather than hear as an effect. The readout always shows the effective rate.
DEPTH · CENTERDEPTH sweeps up to 2.4 octaves; CENTER runs 100 Hz to 8 kHz. At DEPTH zero the sweep parks, leaving a fixed set of notches you place by ear with CENTER: two on JFET and OPTICAL, six on DUAL.
REGEN0 to 95% resonance, soft-limited inside the feedback loop, with high-frequency damping that tightens from 6 kHz to 2.5 kHz as WARMTH rises. It gets sharp and vocal; it never turns into the thin digital scream.
WARMTHTurns the component model up. It is not an EQ. It widens the mismatch between stages, deepens the JFET nonlinearity, drives the feedback loop harder and tightens its damping, then adds a gentle second-harmonic tilt to the wet signal. A −6 dBFS, 1 kHz tone at full WARMTH measures 0.35% THD.
BODYA phase-coherent split at 150 Hz. Turn it down and less of the low end goes through, so a low fundamental barely moves while everything above it keeps swirling. On TWELVE POLE, a 62 Hz fundamental moves more than 20 dB across the sweep at BODY 100, about 5 dB at BODY 55. 2.2 kHz keeps moving about 12 dB throughout. The riff keeps its punch.
MIX · INVERTA linear crossfade, where 50% is the classic full-phase sound. INVERT flips the wet polarity, so peaks become notches.
Auto level

Judge the phasing, not the volume.

A phaser gets louder as REGEN climbs and loses level in its notches as MIX rises, so an A/B made while you dial it in is rigged toward the louder setting. AUTO LEVEL matches the output back to the level coming in and holds a −0.3 dBFS ceiling.

Take REGEN from 0 to 90 and the output moves 3 dB with AUTO LEVEL off, and about a tenth of a dB with it on. Changing mode fades over 10 ms, and nothing reads ahead, so GIRE stays at zero latency. OFF is the default, so sessions saved before 1.1.0 sound exactly as they did.

TRANSPARENT

Rides the gain slowly and leaves the sound alone until the ceiling.

GLUE

Adds a gentle 2.5:1 compressor, the way a bus compressor sits on a mix.

CLIP

Drives 9 dB into the ceiling. Loud on purpose.

Two channels

No spread knob. Each engine sets its own.

The right channel runs the same sweep a fixed distance ahead of the left: 10 degrees on JFET, 12 on OPTICAL, 15 on DUAL. It sweeps around a centre 1.5% higher, with its own component calibration and, in OPTICAL, its own lamp and cell.

Neither channel is ever inverted against the other, so at ordinary settings a fold to mono costs you the image, not the signal. At default JFET settings a mono fold keeps 99.6% of a channel’s level. Pushed hard it costs more: DUAL at full depth and 90% regen keeps about three quarters on average.

Mono in, stereo out opens a single guitar into a phased image, with the left and right sweeps running a few degrees apart around slightly different centres.

The phase terrain

The picture is not an animation.

Each of its 32 rows is one tone burst at one frequency, on a log scale from 60 Hz at the front to 8 kHz at the back. The circuit you are running bends each burst: the crests shift by the phase lag at that frequency, and the envelope slides by the group delay. A notch shows as a flattened cyan row, a resonance as an amber ridge that turns rose as it builds.

MIX pulls the stack apart: DRY at the back, the network’s first pass in front of it, OUT at the front with the right channel printed over it at half density. The stage frequencies and loop gain come from what the engine ran in the last block, not from where the knobs point.

Drag the picture down to flatten the rows toward edge-on, up or double-click to stand it back up. A test in our evaluation harness holds the display against the plug-in’s measured impulse response at 60 parked settings; the worst case today is 3.87%.

Export IR

Print the network as it sits.

The button under the picture prints the network as it sits right now into an impulse response: a 32-bit float WAV at the host’s sample rate, mono or stereo to match the track, ready for any convolver. It offers a name built from the preset, the engine and the rate, and never writes over another file. Exporting never changes a sample of the audio that is playing.

The licence

Fourteen days, the whole plug-in.

The trial is everything, and the header counts down the days left. After that, a computer that has not been activated goes silent until a licence is activated. In GarageBand and other sandboxed hosts it keeps playing.

Activation needs an internet connection once. After that GIRE runs offline, and the licence does not expire. One licence covers three computers, and DEACTIVATE THIS COMPUTER frees a seat so you can move without writing to us.

Once activated, GIRE is never silenced by a licence check. After a hardware change it keeps playing, and the header reads REACTIVATE until you enter your key again.
