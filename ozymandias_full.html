OZYMANDIAS

Singularity processor

RETURN_TO_LAB STORE

A mastering chain with only two surface knobs. RUIN cleans and warms: dynamic resonance suppression, de-harshing, low-mid tightening, and warm saturation that tames transients instead of spiking them. REMNANT weighs, opens and glues.

Most mixes don't need more processing. They need the right processing, in the right order, at the right depth, the part that takes fifteen years to learn and eight plug-ins to execute.

One knob removes what's wrong. One knob adds what's missing. That's the surface. Underneath sit eight studio-grade processors, and a voicing decides how they're proportioned for the record you're making, so the part you actually touch stays two knobs, all the way to the master.

What makes two knobs enough is the preset system. A voicing, HOUSE or ROCK or JAZZ or METAL, doesn't lock in a sound; it sets how much of each processor the macros will call on for that kind of material. Load the one that fits your record, then work entirely from the two dials. Every stage stays available if you want to build a voicing of your own, and every one of them is dynamic: they work flawed material and leave good material alone.

HOW_YOU_USE_IT

01
Load a voicing

Named after the music you're making: HOUSE, JAZZ, METAL. The preset doesn't set a sound. It sets the proportions, how much of each processor the two knobs will call on for that kind of material.

02
Turn RUIN until the mix stops fighting you

Resonance, harshness and low-mid mud leave in a considered order, and warmth arrives last. Most records land somewhere between 40% and 70%.

03
Turn REMNANT until it sounds finished

Weight, then air, then glue. This is the knob that turns a mix into a record.

04
Set the threshold with HORIZON, if you want loud

It starts at 0 dB, fully open, catching only what would clip. Turn it down and the limiter works harder while the level holds, so the record gets denser rather than quieter. Loudness is a decision, never a side effect of the other two knobs.

The advanced panel is there when you want it, not because you need it. Ten controls drop down from the top, each one a proportion the macros multiply into. Open it to build your own voicing, or never open it at all and ride the two knobs. Both are the intended way to use this plug-in.

WHY_TWO_KNOBS_IS_ENOUGH

The macro is intensity. The preset is proportion.

Every advanced control is a weight, not a value: what you hear is the trim multiplied by how far the macro has travelled. That one idea is what makes a preset meaningful. Loading METAL doesn't stamp a sound onto your track, it tells RUIN to lean hard on resonance and harshness control, and then gets out of the way. Turn the knob to 40% and you get 40% of that idea. Nothing about the recipe changes as you move.

Staggered arrival, not lockstep

Each stage enters the sweep at its own point, ordered by how audible the move is. RUIN opens with resonance suppression, the most invisible thing you can do to a mix, and only starts adding saturation past 30%. REMNANT starts with weight, adds air, then glue. Turn either knob slowly and you hear a considered sequence of decisions, because that's exactly what it is.

Dynamic, not static

Almost nothing here is a fixed EQ curve. The suppressor engages only where a band genuinely stands out; the de-harsher works only when the presence region is actually hot; the low-mid bells pull sustained boom while letting kick transients through untouched. A well-made mix at 100% measures nearly flat in the midrange, because there was nothing to correct. A flawed one gets worked. That's why one voicing survives a whole album.

An order of operations you can't stack yourself

Everything subtractive runs before everything additive; everything additive runs before the dynamics. Cleaning before saturating means harmonics grow from a balanced spectrum instead of from mud. Shelving before compressing means the compressor glues the bass you added rather than fighting it. Getting this order wrong is the most common reason a DIY chain sounds worse than its parts.

It tells you the truth

At zero, both macros are a measured null, not “almost”. The makeup gain compensates only the level a stage deterministically removes; level taken off genuinely flawed material stays off, because that reduction is the point. Nothing here quietly turns you up to make the A/B flatter you.

UNDER_THE_HOOD

You don't need this section to use the plug-in. It's here because the people who want it really want it.

RUIN // WHAT_LEAVES
EROSION Dynamic resonance suppression · enters at 0%

Twelve half-octave bands from 180 Hz to 8.1 kHz watch for a band standing proud of its neighbours, a boxy room mode, a honky guitar note, a ringing snare shell, and pull that band alone, in proportion to how far it stands out, releasing the moment it stops offending. You hear nothing, ideally: bypass sounds cluttered, engaged sounds like the mix got out of its own way.

12 bands · Q 2.5 · engages 2 dB above local spectral contrast · 2 dB of cut per dB of excess · up to 8 dB per band, 4 dB in the lowest three · guarded so a dominant band and its skirts are recognised as the music and left alone

FRACTURE De-harshing · enters at 8%

A dynamic bell at 3.3 kHz for presence-range hardness and a shelf above 6 kHz for splash and digital edge, both engaging on level with a small permanent floor. You hear: the mix stops fatiguing you at volume, with sibilance tamed rather than lisped away.

Presence bell 3.3 kHz Q 1.2 to −6 dB · splash shelf above 6 kHz to −4 dB · driven by band-versus-full-band level · sibilance retention verified at 55% or better

BURIAL Low-mid tightening and rumble · enters at 15%

Dynamic bells at 230 Hz and 130 Hz pull sustained low-mid weight with a deliberately slow 20 ms attack, so the first instants of a kick pass at full size while only the sustain behind them is reined in. A sliding highpass removes subsonic rumble that eats headroom without ever being heard. You hear: low end that tightens without thinning.

Dynamic bells 230 Hz to −6 dB and 130 Hz to −4 dB · 20 ms attack / 200 ms release · rumble highpass sliding 16 → 28 Hz with depth

HEAT Warm saturation · enters at 30%

An asymmetric valve-style curve fed a low-frequency-weighted signal, so harmonics grow out of the body of the music rather than its top end. The curve is compressive, so peaks standing above the programme are shaved rather than accentuated, and the bias point breathes with level the way a real gain stage does. You hear: weight and glue without brightness.

Asymmetric tanh with even-harmonic bias · 2× oversampled with anti-derivative anti-aliasing, alias residue below −60 dB · level-dependent bias sag · calibrated so 0 VU sits in the gentle part of the knee · approximately 1% THD at nominal, 1–3 dB of drum-crest reduction

REMNANT // WHAT_ARRIVES
CORE Low-end weight, with restraint · enters at 0%

A weight shelf centred around 90 Hz adding up to 6 dB, but it watches your source first, winding the boost back by up to half when the track is already heavy down there. You get the same impression of weight on a thin mix and a fat one, without turning the fat one into wool.

Second-order shelf, minimum phase so kicks don't pre-ring · to +6 dB · input-keyed adaptive wind-back to 50% as the low band approaches reference level

HALO Air · enters at 10%

A high shelf around 11.5 kHz adding up to 5 dB of open top. Deliberately static and deliberately clean, with no exciter and no generated harmonics. Harshness is FRACTURE's job upstream and level is GRAVITY's downstream; an air band that tried to manage those would fight both. You hear: distance and sheen, with nothing hissing.

Second-order air shelf · to +5 dB · static, harmonics-free · corner placed clear of the de-harsher's working range

GRAVITY Glue compression · enters at 20%

A bus compressor in the classic sense: gentle ratio, soft knee, slow attack so transients survive, and a release that reads the programme, recovering quickly after a brief peak and holding a long tail under sustained pressure. Its detector ignores deep bass, so the low end CORE just added can't pump the mix. You hear: stems starting to sound like one record.

Feed-forward, log-domain, 6 dB soft knee · linked RMS detection with a 90 Hz sidechain highpass · threshold −10 to −18 dBFS, ratio to approximately 2.2:1 · 25 ms attack · program-dependent dual release · gain reduction capped at 8 dB

THE_BUS

HORIZON The limiter's threshold, below a fixed ceiling · in dB · always armed

A brickwall limiter with a fixed −1 dBFS ceiling, always in circuit. The knob is the threshold, measured down from that ceiling, and it starts at 0 dB at the top of the travel, fully open, catching only peaks that would cross. Turn it down and the threshold falls while the makeup rises with it, so peaks stay pinned to the ceiling and the programme gets denser instead of quieter. Gain reduction grows as the knob falls, the way a threshold reads on any limiter you have used.

Why it's on the bus and not the macro: loudness should be a decision, not a side effect. A ring reads the depth at a glance, cool as it leaves the detent at 0 dB, hot when you are leaning on it.

−20 to 0 dB below the ceiling · zero lookahead, therefore zero reported latency · fixed −1 dBFS ceiling held to 0.01 dB · 0.15 ms attack · release adapts to gain-reduction history · composes with OUTPUT, which stays the level trim

MIX & OUTPUT Parallel blend and final level

MIX blends the processed signal against the untouched original, phase-aligned so partial settings never comb: true parallel compression, since the compressor sits before the blend and the limiter after it. OUTPUT sets the final level and doubles as the drive into HORIZON.

  • VoicingsHOUSE, HIP-HOP, ROCK, METAL, POP, JAZZ, ACOUSTIC, CINEMATIC. Each sets the proportions rather than a fixed sound.
  • Resonance suppression12 half-octave dynamic bands, 180 Hz to 8.1 kHz, up to 8 dB per band.
  • Saturation2× oversampled, anti-derivative anti-aliased, with level-dependent bias sag.
