How it works

A noise reducer has to answer one question, over and over, many times a second: is this part of the signal the room, or the voice? CATHARSIS answers it by keeping a running estimate of the room's own level, its noise floor, and comparing everything it hears against it. What sits close to the floor is room and gets turned down. What stands well above it is voice and passes untouched.

Both engines work that way. They differ in how finely they look and what that costs. MULTIBAND splits the signal into twelve bands and adds no delay at all. SPECTRAL looks at 257 frequency bins at a time and needs 512 samples to do it.

AMOUNT sets how far down the room can go, up to 30 dB. It starts at 0, where both engines are exactly transparent: a fresh instance passes audio untouched until you turn it up. AMBIENCE sets how much of the room to leave, and MODE chooses between tracking the floor live and using a profile you captured with LEARN NOISE.

Detection is stereo-linked in both engines. One decision drives both channels, so the room tone that is left cannot wander across the image. A click-free BYPASS crossfades against the dry signal, delayed to match.

    MULTIBAND splits the signal into twelve bands. SPECTRAL takes it apart into 257 bins.

  2. 2TRACK

    Each band or bin keeps an estimate of the room's floor, followed live or captured with LEARN NOISE.

  3. 3DECIDE

    What sits near the floor is turned down, as far as AMOUNT allows. What stands above it passes.

  4. 4KEEP

    AMBIENCE stops the room at a set level below its own floor, so gaps keep a quiet, steady bed.

  5. 5COMPARE

    BYPASS crossfades against the delay-matched dry signal, so A/B never clicks in either engine.

Multiband · zero latency

MULTIBAND is a twelve-band downward expander. Eleven crossovers, at 60, 120, 250 and 500 Hz, 0.9, 1.5, 2.5, 4, 6.3, 10 and 14 kHz, split the signal so that the twelve bands add back up to the input exactly. Nothing is delayed and nothing is smeared in phase, so with AMOUNT at 0 the output is the input, bit for bit.

Each band follows its own level and keeps a noise floor: the lowest that level has been over about the last second. SENS sets a threshold that many decibels above the floor. When the band falls below it, it is turned down by as much as it fell short, up to AMOUNT. It only ever cuts: a band is never raised.

On a steady room, the reduction you get is the smaller of AMOUNT and SENS. The room sits right on its floor, a full SENS below the threshold, so for constant hiss SENS sets the depth and AMOUNT caps it. Raise both together for more.

The twelve band handles on the display set how much of AMOUNT each band uses: drag one up for more, down for less, double-click to bypass it. Shift-click a handle to LISTEN to exactly what that band is taking away. SUGGEST reads the measured floor and sets SENS, AMOUNT and the handles for you, leaning harder on the noisiest bands.

Spectral · bin by bin

SPECTRAL works on 257 frequency bins at once, from a 512-point transform with half-overlapping frames. Every bin carries its own estimate of the room's power, from a tracker that weighs, frame by frame, how likely it is that speech is present in that bin. It follows a changing room within a fraction of a second, without mistaking held vowels for noise.

The gain for each bin is the Ephraim-Malah log-spectral amplitude estimator, the classical answer to the warbling "musical noise" a plain spectral subtractor leaves behind. CATHARSIS weights it by that speech-presence probability, so bins that are almost certainly room go straight to the floor, and bins that might be voice are given the benefit of the doubt.

The floor is not flat. AMOUNT digs deeper where noise lives and speech does not, under 100 Hz and above 10 kHz, and holds back across 250 Hz to 3 kHz, where intelligibility lives. The curve is smooth in frequency, because a step in a gain floor is itself something the ear can find.

The engine reports 512 samples, 10.7 ms at 48 kHz, and the host compensates. Measured at AMOUNT 24 with a captured profile: gaps down 25 dB, words down 0.2 dB.

The estimator that minimises error in the log spectrum, which is close to how loudness is heard. It tracks a smoothed "a-priori" signal-to-noise ratio per bin instead of reacting to every frame's raw value, which is what stops bins flickering on and off.

OM-LSASPEECH PRESENCE

The gain is raised to the power of the probability that speech is present, and the floor fills the rest. A bin that is surely room lands on the floor; a bin that might be a consonant keeps most of its level. The probability has its own fast smoothing, so it follows onsets.

THE CURE FOR LOW-SNR WARBLE
CEPSTRALSMOOTHING

The finished gain is smoothed over time in the cepstral domain. The spectral envelope and the voice's pitch move freely; the random bin-to-bin flicker between them, which is what musical noise is made of, is held steady. It costs two transforms per frame and no latency.

Keep the room

Clean dialogue over dead silence is the giveaway of a denoised recording. Real rooms have a sound, and when it vanishes in every gap the edit is audible. AMBIENCE sets how much of the room to leave, in decibels below the room's own level.

The bed is referenced to the noise floor CATHARSIS is tracking, not to what is playing. So it has the real room's tone and shape, it holds still while the voice comes and goes, and it is independent of AMOUNT: suppress as hard as you like and still land on a defined, quiet room.

That is different from MIX. MIX brings back the dry signal, so the room returns with every movement the processing made, rising under each word and falling in each gap. AMBIENCE returns a bed that does not move. Try both in the lab above: set AMBIENCE to −20 dB and watch the gold band output settle on a steady line in the gaps.

AMBIENCE works in both engines. Off by default, and nothing is synthesised: the bed is the real room, at a lower level.

Auto, learn and suggest

In AUTO, CATHARSIS follows the room as it changes, which suits a moving camera, a scene that cuts between locations, or a fan that cycles. In LEARN, it uses a profile you captured, which suits a fixed room where the noise is steady and you want the reduction to stay exactly where you set it.

To capture one, play a passage of room with nobody speaking and press LEARN NOISE. MULTIBAND listens for about 1.5 s and SPECTRAL for about 0.5 s. The profile is saved with the session, survives buffer and sample-rate changes and offline bounces, and is not written into presets.

  • AUTOA live floor per band (the minimum over about a second) or per bin (a speech-presence tracker). Nothing to set up.
  • LEARNExpands against the captured profile in both engines. Until a capture exists, LEARN tracks live.
  • SUGGESTOne click: SENS 6 dB, AMOUNT 12 dB, and each band handle set between 55 and 100 % by how noisy that band measured. MULTIBAND only.
  • ENGINESwitching fades out, swaps, waits for the new engine to fill and fades back in, and tells the host the new latency. ENGINE is not automatable, by design: no host can move delay compensation in the middle of a pass. Choose it before you play or bounce.
  • VIEWThree skins, LIGHT, DARK and NUCLEAR, kept as your preference and never in presets or sessions. Presets save and save as from the plug-in's own menu.
