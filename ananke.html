How it works

A compressor turns a signal down when it gets loud. A detector measures the level, a gain computer decides how much to take off above the threshold, and an envelope decides how quickly that happens and how quickly it lets go. Every compressor makes the same trade: grab fast enough to hold the peaks and you flatten the transients that made the sound alive.

ANANKE splits that trade into separate controls. The main path rides the level. RELIEF loosens it only during a transient, CREST adds a fast peak-only path above it, and RANGE sets the most it may ever take.

The audio is always delayed by a fixed 3.5 ms, and the detector listens to the undelayed signal, so LOOKAHEAD just moves where the gain lands inside that budget. The latency the host sees never changes when you turn it.

After the gain, EMBER adds analog colour, or colours the input before it is compressed. MIX blends with the time-aligned dry, MAKEUP and AUTO GAIN restore level, and a 5 ms crossfaded BYPASS compares without a click.

  1. 1KEY

    The detector hears the input or an external sidechain, shaped by a high pass, a low pass and a bell.

  2. 2DETECT

    Peak and RMS blend into one level. More RMS rides the phrase and lets the crest go by.

  3. 3COMPUTE

    Threshold, ratio up to ∞:1 and a soft knee set the gain reduction the level asks for.

  4. 4MOVE

    Attack, release or AUTO, and HOLD set the motion. RANGE caps it, RELIEF eases it, CREST backs it up.

  5. 5COLOUR

    EMBER adds harmonics before or after the gain, then MIX, MAKEUP and a click-free BYPASS.

The compressor

The gain computer is a classic soft knee. Below the knee the signal passes untouched. Above it, every dB over the threshold comes out as 1/RATIO of a dB, and across the knee the two meet on a smooth curve, KNEE dB wide. RATIO runs from 1:1 to ∞:1, so the top of the knob is a limiter.

RANGE is a floor on the gain reduction: at its default of −6 dB, ANANKE can never take more than 6 dB, whatever the ratio says. That makes a high ratio safe to reach for. Pull RANGE to the bottom for unlimited reduction.

The envelope is a smooth, decoupled peak detector (Giannoulis and Reiss): ATTACK and RELEASE act separately, so a long release never slows the attack. AUTO REL makes the release depend on the program: light, brief reduction recovers in 40 ms, deep sustained reduction in up to 500 ms, blended on a slow average of how hard it has been working.

Linked detection keys every channel from the loudest, so the stereo image never shifts. Each VOICE rescales the timing and knee you have set (below). On the graph, the transfer curve is drawn the way the plug-in draws it, and a dot rides it at the current level.

Relief and crest

RELIEF is the "slam without the clamp" control. A fast and a slow peak follower on the key give a measure of how transient the signal is right now, independent of its level. While a transient passes, the gain reduction actually applied is eased by RELIEF times that measure. Between hits the measure falls back to zero, so sustained compression, its depth and its pump stay exactly as set. Only the hard clamp on each attack relaxes.

That is not the same as a slower attack. The envelope inside keeps its timing, so release, AUTO REL and HOLD behave as before. RELIEF at 0 is bit-identical to no RELIEF.

CREST adds a second detector: pure peak, a hard knee, the same ratio, and a 0.15 ms attack. It only engages when a peak climbs more than CREST dB above the threshold, so the main path can ride the level gently on RMS while the fast path catches the plosive or the rim shot that would get through. The two combine on whichever asks for more, and RANGE caps both. At the top of its travel CREST is off, an exact null.

DETECT blends the main detector between peak and RMS, 45 % RMS by default. LOOKAHEAD moves the gain up to 3.5 ms ahead of the audio. HOLD freezes the envelope after the last attack before it releases.

Ember character

EMBER is ANANKE's colour stage, decoupled from the dynamics. DRIVE sets how much, TYPE sets the voice, and ROUTING puts it POST the gain, colouring the transients that survive compression, or PRE, colouring the input so the compressor reacts to the harmonics. DRIVE starts at 0, and at 0 the whole stage is skipped: bit-identical to the uncoloured compressor.

The shaper is anti-aliased by first-order ADAA on its residual only, the part it adds, so the clean signal stays flat to the top of the band and there is no oversampling and no added latency. A 5 Hz DC blocker strips the offset the asymmetric curves create.

Like a transformer, TUBE and DIODE drive the lows harder: a low shelf at 150 Hz pushes them into the shaper up to 5 dB hot, and a matching shelf after it takes back half, so the bottom thickens before it gets louder. TUBE adds cathode sag, a linked bloom that lets the bias breathe with the level. BRIGHT adds a 12 kHz air lift instead.

Makeup is matched on the fundamental at half power, so an A/B with DRIVE is not just louder. Changing TYPE while it is audible ducks the colour out, swaps, and ramps back: no click. CHARACTER DELTA plays only what EMBER adds.

Sidechain and stereo

The key the detector hears can be shaped without touching the audio. The SC HPF is on by default at 85 Hz, so a kick or a rumble does not pump a whole vocal or bus. Add the SC LPF to keep hats and sibilance out of it, and the SC EQ bell to make the compressor react to one region, or ignore it.

Flip EXT SC and the detector keys off the sidechain input instead, for ducking a bed under a voice or a bass under a kick. LISTEN plays the key, filters and all, so you hear exactly what the detector hears.

  • SC FILTERSHPF 20–400 Hz (off below 25 Hz) · LPF 200 Hz–20 kHz · bell 200 Hz–8 kHz, ±12 dB
  • EXT SCKey from the host's sidechain bus, mono or stereo. An explicit switch, so connecting a bus never changes the sound by itself.
  • LINK100 % keys every channel from the loudest, one gain for all, no image shift. Lower it toward dual mono and each side starts to answer to itself.
  • MID-SIDECompress mid and side instead of left and right. LINK then blends detection between M and S: hold the centre steady and let the sides breathe.
Hear what it does

Compression is easy to overdo because the ear adapts to it. ANANKE gives you three ways to hear only the change. DELTA plays what the compressor removes, time-aligned: if you hear the body of the vocal in there, it is working too hard. CHARACTER DELTA plays only the harmonics EMBER adds. SC LISTEN plays the detector's key.

The metering sits across the top: the transfer curve with its knee, and a scrolling gain-reduction history you can drag to zoom in time, next to an integrated loudness readout to BS.1770-4.

  • A / BTwo full settings slots and COPY, to compare two approaches without losing either.
  • PRESETSSave and save as, in the plug-in's own menu. DEFAULT is always there to return to.
  • AUTO GAINAdds half the reduction a 0 dBFS peak would see, capped by RANGE, so bypass comparisons stay fair. MAKEUP adds on top.
  • BYPASSA 5 ms crossfade that keeps the delay, so it never clicks and never moves the track in time. The host's own bypass drives it too, and the detector keeps running, so it comes back at the right gain.
  • VIEWResizable window. Three skins: LIGHT, DARK and NUCLEAR. Oversampling OFF, 2× or 4× around the whole compressor.

Who it is for
DIALOGUE & POST

ANANKE began as the final stage of a dialogue channel strip. RMS-leaning detection rides the syllable, CREST catches the plosive, and the image never shifts.

VOCALS

WARM and AUTO REL for a level that sits without pumping, RELIEF to keep the consonants alive, TUBE post for weight, and DELTA to check you have not taken the body.

DRUMS & BASS

PUNCH, a fast attack and RELIEF up: the kit gets dense and the hits still crack. HOLD and EXT SC duck a bass under the kick; DIODE adds grit.

MIX BUS

Low ratio, wide knee, RANGE at a few dB and the SC HPF on: glue without the low end pumping the bus. MIX for parallel, M/S to hold the centre.
