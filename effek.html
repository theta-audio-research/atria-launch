Primary Chassis // ADM 1024 Rack

The legend, bit for bit. And free, because homage shouldn't have a price tag.

In 1983, most digital delays sounded like calculators. One did not. A little blue rack unit had a warmth, a liquid modulation, and a way of softening transients that players still describe as "analog", and it did it with an architecture nobody else was using: adaptive delta modulation, a one-bit-per-clock codec ticking away at up to a megahertz. Studios kept them for decades. The used-market survivors are pushing forty, with DRAM and pushbutton interlocks from the Reagan administration.

EFFEK is our love letter to that machine. We did not sample it, and we did not approximate its frequency response, we rebuilt the mechanism from the original patent and owner's manual, so the things people love happen in EFFEK for the same reasons they happened in the hardware. PLEASE DON'T SUE ME

FOCUS RACK MODULES
NOT A MODEL: THE MECHANISM

Inside EFFEK there is a real 1-bit adaptive delta modulation codec running at the hardware's true 250 kHz – 1 MHz bit clock.

  • · The RAM is real, each range button switches in its actual memory depth, 250 bits on FLANGE HI up to 256 kilobits on LONG ECHO. Bandwidth is set by the oversampling ratio, not the delay length, so a full second of echo stays as bright as a one-millisecond flange.
  • · One clock rules everything: DELAY FACTOR and the sine LFO both bend a single shared VCO driving encoder, memory addressing and decoder. True varispeed: a continuous tape-style Doppler glide over two octaves. It physically cannot glitch, because there is nothing to interpolate.
  • · The warmth is slope overload, fast transients momentarily outrun the codec's adaptive step size, so edges arrive rounded. The famous softness, happening bit by bit.
  • · The grain breathes, quiet passages ride a fine, signal-correlated granular noise swelling and decaying on the codec's 60 ms syllabic time constant. Not hiss, not dither: the sound of a one-bit machine thinking.
  • · Repeats age like tape generations, feedback is re-encoded through the codec on every pass, behind the hardware's 12 kHz loop filter, at sub-unity gain.
IN TIME, WITHOUT LEAVING 1983

The ADM 1024 never had a sync input. EFFEK has one, and it is the only thing on the panel the hardware could not do, so we built it the one way that doesn't break the machine: HOST SYNC adds no second clock.

The six memory banks tile a continuous span from 0.25 ms to 1024 ms, which means any musical division inside that range has exactly one solution on the real hardware: one bank, one DELAY FACTOR setting. Sync just solves that backwards and sets them. The engine never learns a DAW exists.

  • · One button, no new clutter. SYNC sits above DELAY FACTOR and lights amber from behind. While it is lit, DELAY FACTOR stops being continuous and steps through musical divisions instead, and the range buttons switch themselves to whichever bank that division needs. The knob that always meant "time" still means time.
  • · Straight, dotted and triplet, 1/32 through 1/1.
  • · It will not lie to you. 1024 ms is a hard ceiling, because that is LONG ECHO at full DELAY FACTOR. Divisions the machine physically cannot reach are not offered at all, so the list changes with tempo: at 120 BPM everything up to 1/2 is there; a whole note at that tempo is 2 seconds, and the machine simply doesn't go there.
  • · The lamp shows the truth. The button reads SYNC when idle, warms when armed but the host isn't giving a tempo, and lights with the live division when it's actually locked. The millisecond scale keeps showing the engine's real delay.
  • · Modulation still bends it, on purpose. WIDTH sweeps the same VCO the delay rides, so any modulation detunes the synced time, exactly as it would on the hardware. Perfect grid lives at WIDTH 0; everything above that is you choosing drift. We did not decouple them, because that one shared clock is the whole instrument.
  • · Ride the tempo. Change tempo while it plays and the repeats glide in pitch rather than clicking, because the delay is genuinely varispeed.
THE FRONT PANEL, REBORN

The faceplate is the ADM 1024 you remember, the badge, the lamps, the seven square pushbuttons, the MODULATOR box, the rack ears, redrawn in THETA's deep-water Abyssal palette.

  • · Six range buttons: FLANGE HI / MID / LO, DOUBLE, ECHO SHORT / LONG, spanning 0.25 ms to 1024 ms in the original 4:1 banks, plus the coral INFINITE REPEAT.
  • · Bipolar FEEDBACK: positive or negative regeneration from one centre-tapped pot, for two different flavours of comb.
  • · Bipolar DELAY MIX: SOURCE at noon, normal wet clockwise, phase-inverted wet counter-clockwise. The inverted side is half the classic flange recipes.
  • · MODULATOR: WIDTH and SPEED driving the shared VCO. From subtle chorus shimmer to a full pitch-bending, gargling rollercoaster.
  • · Honest lamps: LIMIT is wired to the actual encoder slope-overload detector and input clip. ACTIVE reads the real post-pot level. The marker on the millisecond scale is the engine's actual delay, live, LFO wobble and all.
  • · OUTPUT: SIM / MONO. The ADM 1024 carried two outputs on its back panel, a normal one and a simulated-stereo one, so EFFEK gives you the switch rather than picking for you. Pushed in lights SIM: the patented phase-shift stage, bass anchored in the middle while mids and highs spread. Out lights MONO: the plain jack, the same decoded signal on both sides. Both legends are backlit, and the switch crossfades, so you can flip it while it plays.
  • · INFINITE REPEAT: freezes the bit RAM, non-deteriorating, while the modulator keeps bending the frozen loop. Sound-on-sound, exactly as the panel promised.
THE BROKEN MODES

Here is the part you will not find in the manual. We owned one of these. And like every owner who ever got curious, we discovered that the range buttons are a mechanical interlock, and mechanical interlocks can be defeated.

  • · ALL BUTTONS IN: jam several range buttons down at once and every selected DRAM bank fights for the same read line. Modifier-click to latch them together and the banks genuinely contend for the bus: the delay you dialed in dominates while fragments of the others punch through as ghost grains and granular stutter. With all six latched, DELAY FACTOR becomes a master time control sweeping the entire 0.25 ms – 1024 ms span on one knob, audibly changing gears at every boundary.
  • · NO BUTTONS: the most elusive easter egg, beloved of the deep-cut crowd. Defeat the interlock so no bank is selected and the codec feeds itself with zero delay, leaving only its adaptive slew behaviour: a genuinely lovely, faintly grainy studio limiter hiding inside a delay pedal. Ships as the factory preset NO BUTTONS LIMITER.
EIGHTEEN WAYS IN

Eighteen factory presets, two or three per memory bank, so every part of the machine has a door. FLANGE JET and FLANGE THROUGH ZERO down in the sub-millisecond comb. FLANGE CHORUS and FLANGE VIBRATO. DOUBLER TIGHT and JAM FLANGE BANKS. SLAPBACK 50S, COMB RESONATOR and DOPPLER SWEEP. TAPE SLAP, GRANULAR ECHO and DUB SIREN. ECHO LONG 1024, ECHO DEGRADED and ECHO WASH out at the top of the range. Plus the three originals, including both broken modes.

Every delay time in them is derived from the machine's own law rather than dialed by ear, so DOPPLER SWEEP really is sitting at 38.4 ms and ECHO LONG 1024 really is at the machine's ceiling.

FROM THE 1983 MANUAL

The original owner's manual's recipes work in EFFEK: for the same reasons they worked on the hardware.

  • · Flange: HI or LO range, feedback near max (either polarity), WIDTH high, SPEED slow.
  • · Doubling: DOUBLE range, DELAY MIX at ±50%, a touch of feedback, gentle modulation.
  • · Vibrato: LO range, small WIDTH, SPEED 2–5 Hz, MIX at ±100%.
  • · Wind resonance, flange range, maximum feedback, DELAY FACTOR around 0.5–0.75, MIX at ±100%. Self-oscillating comb heaven.
