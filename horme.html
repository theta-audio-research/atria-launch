How it works

A transient designer changes level over time instead of over frequency. It watches the envelope of the sound, finds the moment a hit begins and the stretch where it rings out, and turns each of those up or down. A compressor reacts to how loud something is; a transient designer reacts to how the level is moving, so a quiet hit and a loud one get the same treatment.

Run that on a whole drum bus and every part of the kit answers to whichever drum is loudest. HORME splits the signal into six bands first, so the kick's body, the snare's crack and the hats each get detectors of their own.

Each band has three detectors running in decibels: one for the onset, one for how far the sound has fallen below its own recent peak, and one for the late tail. ATTACK, SUSTAIN and DECAY scale them, and the result becomes that band's gain, held to ±12 dB.

The bands sum back together, MIX blends against the dry signal, OUTPUT trims the shaped path only, and a click-free BYPASS compares. Off to the side, DETECTION FOCUS and SHAPING EMPHASIS weigh how much each band is allowed to do.

  1. 1SPLIT

    Five Linkwitz-Riley crossovers cut six bands. Added back together they are the input, sample for sample.

  2. 2DETECT

    Each band tracks its own level in decibels and reads the onset, the fall below peak and the late tail.

  3. 3SHAPE

    ATTACK, SUSTAIN and DECAY turn those readings into gain: positive for more of that part, negative for less.

  4. 4WEIGH

    DETECTION FOCUS and SHAPING EMPHASIS scale each band's gain from zero up to twice as much.

  5. 5BLEND

    MIX sets dry against shaped, OUTPUT trims the shaped side. BYPASS crossfades, so A/B never clicks.

Attack, sustain, decay

ATTACK keys off the gap between a fast envelope (1 ms rise, 50 ms fall) and a slower one (15 ms rise, 150 ms fall). On a hit the fast one jumps ahead, and the size of the gap is the size of the boost or cut. Turn ATTACK up and the front of every hit gets louder; turn it down and hits soften.

SUSTAIN measures how far the band has fallen below its own recent peak, with two trackers that rise at the same 5 ms speed. Because they rise together, an onset never reads as sustain: SUSTAIN cannot fight ATTACK for the same moment.

DECAY reaches further out, comparing a 60 / 520 ms pair to find the long tail. It only joins in once the sound is clearly past its peak, and it stays out while the onset detector is still reading.

Every hand-over between the three is a smooth weight, not a switch, so the gain never steps. Below −55 dB a band is left alone, so HORME does not shape hiss. Press PLAY, move the knobs, and switch to DRY to compare.

The six bands

The crossovers sit at 120 Hz, 400 Hz, 1 kHz, 3 kHz and 8 kHz: kick and sub, the low body, the middle, presence, bite, and air. Each split is a fourth-order Linkwitz-Riley built as a difference of low-passes, so the six slices add back to exactly the signal that went in, with no delay. The dashed line below is that sum.

A fresh HORME is one global SHAPE across all six. Open the MULTIBAND page and you can add bands one at a time: each gets its own ATTACK and SUSTAIN offset on top of the master, and a band you have not switched on passes through untouched.

Aim the detector

DETECTION FOCUS is a high-pass and a low-pass on what HORME listens to, not on what you hear. Aim it at the stick and the shell stays out of the decision; pull the low-pass down and cymbal wash stops driving the shaping. The audio passes unfiltered either way. At its opening corners, 20 Hz and 20 kHz, the focus path is skipped outright.

SHAPING EMPHASIS is two nodes that say how hard the shaper works by frequency. +18 dB doubles the shaping in a band, −18 dB switches it off there, and 0 dB changes nothing. HORME samples the curve at the six band centres, so the six stems are exactly what gets applied. Drag the nodes, and press PLAY above to hear the loop change.

What you see

The SIMPLE page is the gain ribbon at the top of this page: a grey mirror of the input, a green curve for gain added and a blue one for gain taken, six seconds wide with the newest at the right. The curves are weighted by how loud each band is, so a band that is barely playing does not drag the reading about. The strip at the left answers which band is doing it, with a two-second peak tick on each.

Under it, on its own axis, is the frequency strip with the focus rings and the emphasis nodes.
