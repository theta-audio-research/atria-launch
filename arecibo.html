Polyphonic synthesizer · In development

ARECIBO

ARECIBO is a polyphonic synthesizer that speaks in receiver language. Two oscillators and five layers make the voice; a transmission sequencer, a glitch engine and a fragment engine break it up; ring modulation, delay and a rack of spaces carry it out.

Everything plays from the keyboard. Hold a chord and the sequencer moves the whole chord in key. Turn one face knob and several parameters move together, always from the sound you saved.

The voice

Each voice starts with two oscillators: sine, warm, saw, pulse, triangle or noise, each with its own shape, level, octave and tune. Oscillator B can frequency-modulate A or ring it. You drag the wave picture itself to change its shape.

Under them sit five layers with their own faders: a sub an octave down, a stack at a fixed interval up to two octaves, coloured noise, air, and a cloud of detuned partials that beats slowly against the note.

Up to 16 voices, played poly, mono or legato, with glide and a unison fan you widen and detune by dragging. A single filter switches between low-pass, band-pass, high-pass and notch, with drive, key tracking and velocity.

Two envelopes, two LFOs with six shapes including sample-and-hold and a smooth drift, mod wheel and pressure, and a twelve-route modulation matrix: drag a handle from the shelf onto any knob to make a route.

Interference and the transmission sequencer

Interference is a glitch engine that does not wobble. Every event starts on the grid, does its job and gets out of the way. The five bars on the page are the controls: drag them to set how often each kind of event fires.

The transmission sequencer has sixteen steps. Held keys are the roots: each step strikes every held note, moved by its scale degree and snapped back into key, so a chord sequences as a chord. Lock it to the host and the pattern sits on the bar; leave it free and it starts from the first key you play.

  • STUTTER · RATCHETCaptures a slice from 1/4 to 1/64 of a beat and repeats it. Ratchet halves the slice on every repeat.
  • LOSSRhythmic dropouts, gate-chopped on an eighth or sixteenth grid with millisecond fades.
  • BITSBursts of decimation and bit reduction, redrawn every sixteenth so it crackles rather than hums.
  • SCAN · REVERSE · STOPScale-locked pitch jumps, the audio just played run backwards, and a tape-stop pull-down.
  • FRAGMENT · SHATTERSequencer steps that hand the signal to DIFFERENTIAL for their length.
DIFFERENTIAL inside

The fragment engine from DIFFERENTIAL runs inside ARECIBO, after Interference. Pick a character, then push it with four macros: Chop, Repeat, Motion and Crush, plus a bipolar Colour and Mix.

Run it on the whole mix, or inside every voice, so each note fragments on its own. Eight slot cards can each take an operation of their own, with Tone and Drive.

Beside it, Bounce lays one of sixteen bouncing-gap patterns over every bar of the host. Switched off, both are exact bypasses with no latency.

Ring modulation and delay

The FX page carries two THETA plug-ins reduced to a synth player's controls. The ring modulator comes from RING BEARER: a carrier that bends from sine to a band-limited square, a slow swing on its pitch, a crossover that keeps the lows clean, and drive. Set it to track and the carrier follows the note you play.

The delay comes from BUCEPHALUS: free or host-synced time, feedback to 92%, grain or reverse in the loop, width, tone and Freeze. The repeats sit after the echo cloud, so they carry the whole sound.

The space rack

Eight modules sit in a rack, each with its own power light. Click one and it opens large beside the list, with its picture as the control.

  • ENSEMBLEA warm, wandering chorus with three taps a side: Chorus, Wide, or String for the dense shimmer of string machines. The lows stay solid and centred.
  • DRIFT · BREATHESlow pitch drift across the voices, and a swell that breathes in and out.
  • FIELDA room you shape by dragging its corner, with pre-delay, damping and shimmer.
  • PULSE · PANA synced volume gate and stereo movement.
  • TAPEA worn transport on the whole output: wow, drift, flutter, age, hiss and dust. Below about 120 Hz the motion is shared, so the sub stays steady.
  • EVOLVESlowly drifts the colour of the patch (filter, shapes, FM, cloud, detune and drive) while pitch, envelopes and timing stay where you left them. Every move shows on the panel.
Macros, XY and Dice

Seven face knobs: Signal, Texture, Interference, Tape, Field, Drift and Motion. Each moves several parameters at once. At the centre the patch is exactly as saved; turn right to add, left to take away. Turn Drift up and a module that was off engages, fading in from silence.

The XY · Dice pad scans two macros at once. It can move on its own (orbit, figure eight, bounce, wander) in time with the host, or follow a path you draw on it with REC.

Along the top: Dice rolls a new sound around the one you have, Variation nudges it, and Undo steps back to where you were. Keep tuning holds your pitch settings while you explore.

Capture records what you play straight to a WAV file.
