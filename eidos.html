How it works

A resonance is a frequency that rings longer and louder than the ones around it: the mode of a small room, the honk of a cheap mic, a cymbal's whistle, a voice's edge at 3 kHz. A static EQ cut fixes it everywhere, including the moments it was fine. EIDOS cuts a frequency only by the amount it stands above its own neighbourhood, and only while it does.

Every frame, EIDOS compares each frequency with the average level of the spectrum around it, its local envelope. The difference is the excess. Whatever excess clears the bar is taken back, scaled by DEPTH and by the five bands.

DETAIL sets how wide that neighbourhood is, from about an octave down to a sixth of one. SELECT raises the bar, so only the worst offenders are touched. FOCUS spares transients and saves the cut for resonances that persist, and ATTACK and RELEASE set how fast a cut grabs and lets go.

The cut is applied one of three ways, picked on the LATENCY row. DELTA plays only what was removed, so you can hear exactly what EIDOS is taking.

  1. 1MEASURE

    The input is cut into overlapping frames and turned into a spectrum, or run through 32 band filters in LL.

  2. 2COMPARE

    Each frequency is held against the average of its neighbourhood. What sticks out is the excess.

  3. 3WEIGHT

    DEPTH and the five bands decide how much of that excess may go. SELECT and FOCUS decide which.

  4. 4SMOOTH

    ATTACK and RELEASE shape every cut in time, so the gain never zips or pumps.

  5. 5APPLY

    The cut lands in linear phase, minimum phase or zero latency. Cut only: the gain never goes above 1.

Excess over the envelope

EIDOS does not look for loud frequencies. It looks for frequencies that are loud for where they are. For every bin it averages the level, in decibels, of the spectrum around it, a window DETAIL sets from one octave wide at 0 % to a sixth of an octave at 100 %. That average is the local envelope, the gold dashed line below. Anything above it is excess.

In SOFT, the default, a bin has to clear the envelope by half a decibel plus SELECT before anything happens, and then DEPTH × the band weight of the excess is removed. At DEPTH 100 % with a band fully up, a resonance is pulled right down to its envelope, never below.

SELECT raises the bar by up to 24 dB. At 0 it treats every excess, which smooths broadly; turn it up and only the peaks that really stand out get cut. MAX CUT, a host parameter, puts a floor under any single cut, −24 dB by default.

HARD is the aggressive mode: it drops the half-decibel allowance and takes one and a half times the excess, and it also cuts anything that rises over a fixed level line that DEPTH lowers. Its cut is always at least SOFT's on the same material. The scatter on the right shows every bin: its excess across, its cut down.

Focus and ballistics

A snare crack and a ringing snare overtone can stand above their neighbours by the same amount. One is the hit, and you want it. The other is the problem. FOCUS tells them apart by how long they last.

Alongside the live spectrum, EIDOS keeps a slow one, averaged over about a second, and measures the excess there too. A resonance that persists shows up in the slow spectrum; a transient barely registers. As FOCUS rises, each cut is scaled by how much excess the slow spectrum holds at that frequency: sustained rings keep their cut, and hits pass through.

ATTACK, 1 to 200 ms, sets how fast a cut grabs, and RELEASE, 10 to 1000 ms, how long it holds once the resonance stops. The defaults are 15 and 80 ms. A short attack catches an S on its first frame; a long release keeps a room mode from pumping between notes.

The lab runs two frequencies of one source through the detector frame by frame: the RING, which persists, and the HITS, which do not. Turn FOCUS up and watch the hit cuts fall away while the ring's stays.

Three engines, one instance

Every resonance suppressor has to decide where its cut lives in time. EIDOS lets you choose per session, in the same instance, from the LATENCY row.

LINEAR analyses 4096-point frames with 75 % overlap and applies the cut to each frequency without touching its phase: true linear phase, the most precise of the three, at 4096 samples of delay (85 ms at 48 kHz). The cost of linear phase is pre-ring: a narrow cut smears a little energy ahead of the note. On a finished stem or a dialogue bounce, that is the right trade.

MIN runs the same detector on 1024-point frames and turns the cut into its minimum-phase equivalent before applying it: the same depth, nothing ahead of the note, 1024 samples of delay (21 ms).

LL drops frames altogether. Thirty-two band filters, about a third of an octave apart, follow the input sample by sample, and a cascade of 32 dynamic bells applies the cut. No added latency, for tracking, live sound and picture sync. The price is resolution: 32 taps instead of 2049 bins, so a very narrow ring is cut a third of an octave wide.

Switching is click-free: the active engine fades to silence, the swap and the new latency report happen there, and the new engine waits until it is full before fading back in. QUALITY and BYPASS never change the latency, so neither makes the host re-sync.

The bands

The graph is the control surface. Five numbered nodes sit above the midline; together they draw a weight map across the spectrum, the silver line, and EIDOS removes DEPTH × that weight of the excess at every frequency. Where no band reaches, nothing is cut. Each band is a smooth bell on a log-frequency scale: height is its amount, width its spread in octaves.

Drag a node: across for frequency, up for more. Cmd-drag for width. Double-click to bypass it. Hold SHIFT and press a node to LISTEN: EIDOS plays a narrow band around it, and dragging sweeps it, so you can hunt the ring by ear and then drop the band on it.

A fresh instance opens with every band at zero and DEPTH at zero: flat and dry, an exact null.

Modes

The MODES plate sets how EIDOS listens. Every combination works in all three engines.

DETECTSOFT · HARD

SOFT follows the material: the bar is the local envelope plus half a decibel and SELECT. HARD takes one and a half times the excess with no allowance, and adds a fixed level line that DEPTH pulls down, so loud resonances are caught even in dense mixes.

HARD CUTS AT LEAST AS DEEP AS SOFT, EVERYWHERE
STEREOLINKED · M-S

LINKED detects on the mid and applies one cut to both sides, so the image never shifts. M-S runs independent detectors on mid and side, for a ring that lives only in the room or only in the centre. STEREO LINK, a host parameter, blends from dual mono to fully linked.

LINK 0 TO 100 % · DEFAULT 100
SIDECHAININT · EXT

EXT detects on the sidechain input and cuts the main signal. Key a dialogue track's resonance control from its boom, or a reverb return from its dry source. If the host leaves the sidechain unconnected, EIDOS falls back to the main input.

DETECTION ONLY · THE SIDECHAIN IS NEVER HEARD
  • QUALITYECO runs detection every other frame for about 35 to 40 % less CPU. NORM is 75 % overlap. HIGH is 87.5 % overlap, finer in time at about twice the CPU. Latency is the same in all three, and OFFLINE, a host parameter, can force HIGH for bounces.
  • DELTAPlays the removed signal, dry minus wet. If DELTA sounds like the problem, you are done. If it sounds like the performance, back off.
  • BYPASSA true internal bypass: the engine keeps running and the output crossfades over 12 ms to a dry copy delayed by exactly the reported latency. The host's own bypass button drives the same path, so toggling never clicks and never moves the latency.
  • SESSIONA/B with COPY, 64 steps of undo where a whole knob gesture counts as one, and presets that store the sound but never the bypass.
