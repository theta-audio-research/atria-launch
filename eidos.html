A dynamic, cut-only resonance suppressor: EIDOS finds boxiness, ring and harshness the moment they sound, removes only the excess, and gets out of the way. Nothing is notched permanently. Nothing is ever boosted.

Three engines, from linear-phase to zero latency · VST3 · AU · AAX · Standalone for macOS

EIDOS is the centrepiece stage of ROGERIAN, THETA's dialogue channel strip, lifted out and offered as a focused, single-purpose plug-in. It was voiced for dialogue, but resonance doesn't care what it lives on: a vocal that bites, an acoustic guitar with a wolf note, a snare that rings, a cymbal that sears, a room mic with a mode at 140 Hz, a mix with a hard spot — EIDOS treats them all the same way. Only while they sound.

The name: in Greek, eidos (εἶδος) is form — the essential shape of a thing, what remains when everything incidental is stripped away. EIDOS doesn't reshape your sound. It removes what's obscuring it.

HOW_IT_WORKS

CUTS THAT EXIST ONLY WHILE THE PROBLEM DOES

A static EQ notch is a permanent opinion: it removes the frequency whether the problem is happening or not, and hollows out everything that was fine. EIDOS is dynamic and cut-only. It continuously measures each frequency against the signal's own local spectral envelope — how far it stands above its neighbours — and removes a fraction of that excess, at the depth the moment demands, then gets out of the way.

At DEPTH zero the engine is a true null — the output is the input, delayed only by the engine's reported latency — verified by null test, with a residual below −120 dB FS. The zero-latency engine nulls bit-exactly.

STEER IT LIKE AN INSTRUMENT

The hero display is the control surface: five movable bands above the midline, frequency on X, depth on Y — and the live reduction drawn beneath them, a heat-map that deepens as the engine works harder. Not a curve you drew. What's actually being removed, right now.

  • · Drag a band up where it rings — each band is a smooth Gaussian curve, its width set in octaves.
  • · Modifier-drag for width. Double-click to bypass a band.
  • · Hold a band to LISTEN — audition a narrow slice around it and sweep to hunt the resonance by ear: the same gesture engineers have used on an EQ for fifty years, without the EQ's commitment.
  • · DEPTH — the master: how much of the detected excess is removed.
  • · DETAIL — detection sharpness, from broad (about an octave) to surgical (about a sixth of an octave).
  • · FOCUS — persistence weighting. As it rises, sustained ring gets treated and transient consonants get spared.
  • · MIX and OUTPUT — parallel blend and trim.
  • · DELTA — solo exactly what's being removed. The fastest honesty check in the plug-in: if DELTA sounds like the problem, you're done; if it sounds like the performance, back off.
TWO DETECTOR TEMPERAMENTS
  • · SOFT — adaptive. The threshold is the signal's own spectral envelope, so cuts ease resonances down relative to what the material is doing around them.
  • · HARD — absolute. Holds a fixed line for surgical work — a threshold that tracks overall level rather than the envelope around each peak.
REFINE — shape the whole response at once

Four global modifiers fold over the five-band curve without touching the bands themselves:

  • · TILT — bias the suppression toward highs or lows, ±6 dB per octave around 1 kHz.
  • · MAX CUT — a hard ceiling on how deep any cut can go.
  • · BIAS SCALE — scale the entire curve, 0–200%.
  • · BIAS SHIFT — slide every band's centre in semitones, ±24. Retune the whole profile to a new key, a new voice, a new room, in one move.
THREE ENGINES. ONE CONTROL SET.

Resonance work spans tracking to final mix, so EIDOS carries three reconstruction engines behind one control set — pick the tier the session can afford:

  • · LINEAR — the full-resolution engine with a linear-phase reconstruction. The most surgical and the most transparent; the one for a finished stem or an offline pass. (~85 ms at 48 kHz, fully reported.)
  • · MIN — the same detector at a quarter of the window, through a minimum-phase reconstruction: causal, no pre-ring, ~21 ms. Finer than LL, far shorter than LINEAR — the middle path for mixing while monitoring.
  • · LL — a 32-band dynamic filterbank version of the same behaviour at essentially zero latency, for tracking, live use, and picture-sync. Coarser than the STFT engines by design — that is the honest price of zero latency.

These are the same three reconstruction paths that live inside ROGERIAN — there labelled Linear, Natural and Zero; EIDOS names them as its own panel does. Switching engines is click-free: the active path ducks to silence, the swap and the latency re-report happen at the silent point, and the new path ramps in.

STEREO THAT HOLDS ITS IMAGE

The LINEAR engine carries the full channel-coupling set:

  • · LINKED — detection runs on the mid signal and one gain curve is applied identically to both channels, so hard suppression never shifts or smears the stereo image.
  • · M-S — independent engines on Mid and Side. Treat a centred vocal's ring without touching the width, or clean a resonant room from the sides only.
  • · EXT sidechain — detect from another signal entirely and apply the cuts to this one. Duck a dialogue bus's resonances from the boom track; key an instrument's treatment from its close mic. Falls back gracefully if the sidechain isn't connected.

The MIN and LL engines process each channel independently — lower latency, simpler coupling, by design.

A BYPASS THAT BEHAVES

Small thing, done properly: EIDOS's bypass is a latency-compensated crossfade against a sample-aligned dry path — engaging it never clicks, never thumps, and never makes the host re-sync. It's registered with the host as the plug-in's official bypass, so your DAW's own bypass button gets the same smooth behaviour. The processing chain stays warm while bypassed, so un-bypassing is seamless too.

Save, recall, and overwrite your own settings from the preset menu in the header. Presets store the sound, never the bypass state, so recalling one never mutes a live chain. Three interface themes — LIGHT, DARK, and NUCLEAR — in THETA's machined, gold-on-graphite design language.
