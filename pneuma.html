How it works

An equaliser changes how loud each part of the spectrum is. That is all a clean one can do: it moves levels up and down. An analog console EQ did a second thing nobody asked for. Boost a band and you drove that frequency harder into the transformers and tubes behind it, so the boost came back with harmonics of its own. The band you leaned on got warmer, and the rest of the signal did not.

Most plug-ins model that by putting a saturator after the whole EQ, which colours everything at once. PNEUMA gives every band its own.

Each of the sixteen bands has a curve (FREQ, GAIN, Q and a shape), a level detector that can move the gain on its own (ABOVE and BELOW one threshold), and PUSH, which taps the band's region and drives it into the shaper. The bands can each work on stereo, mid, side, left or right.

After the bands, BODY, AIR and SAT add broadband colour, before or after the EQ. OUTPUT trims, and a click-free BYPASS compares.

  1. 1SHAPE

    Each band filters: bell, shelf, cut, notch, tilt, band pass or all pass, on the channel you route it to.

  2. 2MOVE

    The band's detector rides its gain: down when the region gets loud, up when it goes quiet, or both.

  3. 3PUSH

    The band's region, after its own boost or cut, goes through the shaper. Only the harmonics come back.

  4. 4COLOUR

    BODY, AIR and SAT add broadband even and odd harmonics, before or after the bands.

  5. 5TRIM

    OUTPUT sets the level. BYPASS crossfades, so A/B never clicks.

Harmonics on every band

PUSH sits under every band's knobs. Turn it up and PNEUMA taps that band's region with a band pass at the band's own frequency and Q. The tap comes after the band's filter, so a band you have boosted drives harder, the way a console boost hit the iron behind it. That signal goes into the shaper, and PNEUMA keeps only what the shaper added: the shaped signal minus the tap. That residual is the harmonics, and it is added back on top of the band.

Because the dry band is never touched, PUSH 0 is bit-identical to no PUSH at all. A band with PUSH up colours its own region. The low-mid band can thicken while the presence band stays clean, in mid only, or on the sides only.

And you can see it on the curve. Wherever a band's PUSH is adding harmonics, the gold EQ curve blooms in a warm ember across that band's region: wide or narrow with the band's Q, brighter and redder the more PUSH adds, from the same reading as the HARMONICS meter. You see at a glance which bands are being driven and how hard. Nothing glows at PUSH 0 or in linear phase.

The voice is the SATURATION plate's MODE, shared by every band so the whole plug-in speaks one knee: TUBE leans on the second harmonic and gives way to the third when pushed, TAPE is odd-led with softer evens under it, CLIP is purely odd. Click PUSH · TUBE on the band plate to change it with the colour drawer closed.

The shaper runs oversampled, 4× in MAXTONE and 2× in LOWLAT, with antiderivative anti-aliasing at the high rate. Both depths add zero latency: the filters' delay lands on the harmonics only, never on the dry path. Under each PUSH knob a HARMONICS meter reads, in decibels, how much of the band's output PUSH added, running amber to red as the band gets genuinely hot.

Two-way dynamics

Every band has one threshold and two ranges. ABOVE acts when the band's region is louder than the threshold, BELOW when it is quieter, and the two meet smoothly at the threshold, through an 8 dB knee. Set ABOVE negative and BELOW positive and a single band tames the peaks and lifts the tails at the same time.

Each band listens to its own region, on its own routing: a side band reacts to the sides. ATTACK and RELEASE set the ballistics, and ONSET blends a 0.5 ms detector in, so the band answers transients as well as level. Flip DYN KEY to EXT and every band keys off the sidechain input instead.

AUTO, on by default, sets the threshold for you from a slow running average of the band's own level, so a dynamic band works the moment you set a range. Turn it off and THRESHOLD is yours.

BELOW is gated at −70 dB. Without that gate, a lift would grab hold of fades, pre-roll and bare noise floor, because the quieter a signal gets the harder BELOW engages. On the graph a faint slate line shows how far each band's ABOVE range can take it, and a brighter slate line shows where it is right now. On this page the space between the dialed curve and the live one is tinted red where gain is taken and blue where it is given, to make the movement easy to read.

Curve character

One switch reshapes every bell at once, around the frequency, Q and gain you set. NEUTRAL is the standard analog-matched bell. The other six change how a boost spreads, the way different consoles answer the same knob position. The dashed line is NEUTRAL; drag GAIN and watch MUSICAL and A tighten as the boost grows.

MUSICAL · PROPORTIONAL Q
  • NEUTRALThe standard analog-matched bell.
  • COOLA flat-topped plateau built from two bells either side of your frequency, so a boost spreads out and the centre never gets over-loud.
  • SHARPA focused peak: a smaller shoulder at your Q with a tip four and a half times narrower on top.
  • MUSICALProportional Q. Broad at small gains and tightening toward 12 dB, so small moves are gentle bends and big moves stay controlled.
  • NBroad inductor. A widened bell over a wide supporting skirt: the big-iron low-shelf feel on any band.
  • AProportional forward. Tightens with gain harder than MUSICAL and keeps full energy at the centre: the punchy, assertive move.
  • PPassive tilt. A boost carries a shallow counter-dip at 2.2 times its frequency, weight without mud; a cut mirrors it.
The bands

PNEUMA opens on the classic seven: a 25 Hz low cut, a low shelf, three bells, a high shelf and a 12.5 kHz high cut, with every gain at zero. Nine more bands sleep out of sight. Double-click empty graph space and a sleeping band wakes on the analyzer's nearest real resonance, not just where you clicked, and a hairline flashes the frequency it found. Right-click any node to snap it to a peak.

Drag a node to move it. The selected band's strip carries its shape, channel, FREQ, GAIN or SLOPE, Q, the dynamics and PUSH. LISTEN narrows the monitor to one band so you can hunt a resonance by ear.

Body, air and saturation

Three analog modules sit on the plates under the graph. Each has its own switch and its own AMOUNT, none of them gates another, and every AMOUNT starts at 0, so a fresh instance is still an exact null. One switch puts all three before the EQ or after it, crossfaded so it never clicks.

BODYEVEN · LOW MID

Flux-driven even-harmonic warmth with a head-bump bloom: low-end weight that reads as size rather than level. BIAS leans the curve, FREQ sets where the bloom sits.

AMOUNT 0–100 % · BIAS ±1.00 · FREQ 60–600 Hz
SATURATIONBROADBAND

Its own saturator for the whole signal. DRIVE comes with automatic make-up, MODE picks TUBE, TAPE or CLIP (the same voice PUSH uses), HEADROOM calibrates the knee, and IRON adds a very light console-transformer glue. Below 100 Hz its input is high-passed at 12 dB/oct, so SAT never turns the sub into intermodulation mud: the bass stays clean and the mids above it stay clearer. Low-end weight is BODY's job. Felt more than heard: try 20 to 40 %.
